# Nike sweatpants: how to buy Club Fleece, Tech Fleece and Phoenix Fleece for less

Most people searching for Nike sweatpants already know what they want. A pair of joggers, probably Club Fleece, maybe the wider Phoenix silhouette, and the question is really about price. Nike keeps its own site at full retail almost all year, which is why the same pants look dramatically cheaper on Amazon one week and on Zappos the next. The gap isn't random, and it isn't only about sales.

Two things decide what you actually pay: which fleece line the pants came from, and whether the order ran through a cashback layer on the way in. The first you can figure out in about two minutes. The second is where most buyers leave money behind purely because they opened the brand's app instead of a link.

### Four habits that decide whether it tracks

1. **Start the session on the cashback platform, every time.** Not the browser bookmark to the retailer, not a saved cart, and not after browsing the products in another tab first.
2. **Clear the cart and close the retailer's app before you click through.** For Nike specifically, the platforms instruct users to empty the shopping cart first and avoid completing the purchase inside the brand's own app.
3. **Accept the retailer's cookies.** The tracking confirmation depends on it.
4. **Skip ad blockers, VPNs, and unlisted coupon codes.** Facebook or Google ad links, other cashback extensions, and promo codes that aren't featured on the cashback platform can all break attribution. Codes beginning with specific prefixes (on the Singapore merchant page, "CS-" and Family and Friends codes) are explicitly excluded.

Cashback is calculated on the eligible order total, so it stacks on top of Nike's own sale prices and Member pricing rather than replacing them. It also stacks with whatever your credit card gives you, since the two run on separate rails.

## What won't earn cashback

This list is where most disappointment lives, so read it before you buy, not after.

On the US Nike merchant page, exclusions include New Releases, Air Jordans and Air Jordan Retros, Hurley wetsuits, refurbished shoes, HyperIce, HyperAdapt and Adapt BB products, anything bought on the Nike employee site, and everything bought through the SNKRS app. Gift cards and store credit purchases or redemptions don't count, and neither do fees, taxes, shipping or delivery charges.

Singapore adds its own carve-outs: Nike app purchases, SNKRS purchases, Nike By You custom designs, selected football boots, and — an unusual one — orders containing five or more units of the same item. Returns, exchanges, cancellations and refunds all void the cashback, though partial returns are prorated rather than wiped out.

The pattern to remember: hyped releases and custom designs never earn cashback anywhere, because those channels don't allow third-party attribution. Standard Club Fleece joggers in a normal colourway earn it fine.

## When Nike sweatpants actually get cheaper

Nike's own site holds near-full retail for current-season stock. Discounting happens through predictable windows instead.

End-of-season clearance is the most reliable. Nike rotates fleece and outerwear aggressively at the end of winter, and past-season colourways of Club Fleece routinely land in the $40s. Member Days and birthday-month promotions push select items down for signed-up members, and membership is free. Between the big windows, payday flash deals are common — typically in the last week of the month.

Then there's the third-party layer. Amazon, Zappos and sporting-goods retailers discount Nike fleece deeper than Nike does, sometimes by 40% or more on specific colourways. The menswear Club jogger was a top-ten best-selling men's sweatpant on Amazon with over 8,400 ratings averaging 4.7 out of 5 across the run, which tells you something about how much volume moves there. If you're flexible on colour, checking a marketplace before defaulting to the brand's own site is usually worth the two minutes.

The trade-off is straightforward. Buying direct keeps the range, the Member perks and the cashback stack intact. Buying through a marketplace tends to get you a lower sticker price but a different cashback rate, or none at all, and slightly murkier sizing information.

## Sizing and fabric details worth checking first

Nike's oversized cuts genuinely run oversized. The brand's own product page for the women's Club Fleece mid-rise oversized sweatpants says plainly that the style fits large and recommends ordering a size down. Zappos reviews on the Phoenix Fleece high-waisted oversized version echo this — it's the single most repeated complaint in that listing's ratings, where 77% of the 39 reviews gave five stars but the sizing note keeps coming up. One reviewer also flagged a colour mismatch between the photos and the delivered item, which is worth remembering if you're ordering a muted colourway on a no-returns-promo basis.

Fabric weight also varies within a line. The men's Club Fleece open-hem pant lists roughly 80–81% cotton with 19–20% polyester, and Nike notes the percentages can shift slightly by production run. Check the label rather than assuming two Club Fleece styles feel identical, because they sometimes don't.

If you're buying a sweatsuit rather than just pants, matching the same product family matters more than matching the colour name — Club Fleece and Tech Fleece don't read as a set even in the same shade.
